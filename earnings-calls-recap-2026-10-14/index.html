What management said, and where analysts pushed: Q1 and Q2 2026 earnings calls

One page per company on the last two earnings calls for each desk we cover. For each quarter: what management chose to highlight, and where analysts pushed back. Each desk wrote its recap from the call transcripts. Figures are as stated on the calls, quotes are verbatim, and short notes in brackets explain the jargon. The next earnings date closes each section.

Amazon AMZN

Speakers named on the calls include Andy Jassy (CEO) and Brian Olsavsky (CFO).

Q1 2026 · Apr 29

What management highlighted
  • Amazon Web Services (AWS, the cloud business) grew 28%. Jassy called it “the fastest growth rate in 15 quarters.” AWS runs at about $150B a year (“run rate” means the current quarter scaled to a full year); AI alone is over $15B.
  • Amazon’s own chips are now a business of over $20B a year, “now one of the top three data center chip businesses in the world” (Jassy). Customers have committed over $225B to Trainium, Amazon’s AI chip, which will “save us tens of billions of dollars of CapEx each year.”
  • Data-center money goes out “six to 24 months before we start billing customers,” so “the early years free cash flow is challenged.” Q1 capital spending (capex: buildings, servers, equipment) was $43.2B.
  • Retail units sold rose 15%, “the highest we’ve seen since the tail end of COVID lockdowns.” Olsavsky: a 13.1% operating margin, “our highest operating margin ever.” Grocery passed $150B in 2025. Ads were $17.2B, up 22%.
  • Leo, Amazon’s satellite internet, is close to launch and Amazon plans to buy Globalstar. Leo adds about $1B of Q2 costs, plus “higher transportation costs related to fuel inflation.”
Where analysts pushed
  • Sheridan (Goldman) on spending: Jassy, “I don’t have a new update on capital. Our plan is largely the same.”
  • Nowak (Morgan Stanley) on reliance on a few AI labs: the backlog (signed future revenue) is “$364 billion,” not counting a new Anthropic deal over $100B, and it is “not just one customer or two customers.”
  • Shweta Khajuria (Wolfe) on memory-chip costs: prices have “skyrocketed,” but “we’re not capacity constrained there.” On AI shopping agents: “advertising will do well in a world of agentic commerce.”
  • Nowak on outside AI assistants: their traffic is “a small fraction” of search-engine referrals.
  • Post (BofA) on selling Trainium to others: “there’s a good chance we’re gonna sell racks over the next couple years”; customers “will consume as much as we make.”

Q2 2026 · Jul 30

What management highlighted
  • AWS grew 36.7%, “accelerating for the fifth straight quarter.” The backlog reached $496B; AWS runs at about $169B a year, and AI and chips are each over $25B.
  • Spending goes up: “we now believe we will spend approximately $220 billion in cash CapEx in 2026” (up from about $200B, because of memory costs), and “we will still not have enough capacity” in 2026 and 2027.
  • AWS could “very possibly be a trillion-dollar annual revenue business for us in time”; servers take “a little less than three years to break even.”
  • One-time items cut costs by “approximately $1.2 billion” (about $600M of tariff refunds and $600M of energy-contract gains). Operating income was $27.5B.
  • Paid units rose 17%; ads were $19.8B, up 26%; fresh-grocery monthly shoppers are up “over 50% since the start of the year.”
  • Q3 guidance: sales $197–202B, operating income $22.5–26.5B. Prime Day moved into Q2; without that, growth “would have been nearly 400 basis points higher” (4 percentage points).
Where analysts pushed
  • Anmuth (JPMorgan) on AWS margins: up 6.5 points (5.2 without the energy gain); “these margins will fluctuate,” and AI margins are “a little bit ahead” of early cloud.
  • Anmuth on building its own top-tier AI model: Amazon “can have a wildly successful business without its own frontier model,” though “we are pursuing our own frontier model.”
  • Nowak on 2027 spending: no slowdown promised; 2027 capacity is “largely reserved,” with “quite a bit” for 2028.
  • Gawrelski (Wells Fargo) on rising costs vs fixed contracts: signed deals keep their prices, new deals are priced at today’s costs, with “inflated prices right now on some of the components like memory and hard drives and SSDs.”
  • Sebastian (Baird) on how it gets paid for: “you’ve seen us issue debt this year,” “nothing to share today.”
  • Post on why AWS sped up: “there are a lot of other reasons why it’s growing.”

What to watch next (~Oct 29 (estimated)): Whether the $220B spending plan grows again, and whether AWS keeps accelerating without one-time help.

Alphabet GOOGL

Speakers: Sundar Pichai (CEO), Anat Ashkenazi (CFO), Philipp Schindler (Chief Business Officer).

Q1 2026 · Apr 29

What management highlighted
  • Search revenue rose 19%. Pichai said people “love our AI experiences like AI Mode and AI Overviews, and they’re coming back to Search more,” with queries “at an all time high.”
  • Cloud revenue rose 63% to over $20B. The backlog of signed contracts “nearly doubled quarter on quarter to over $460 billion,” and operating margin went from 17.8% to 32.9% (Ashkenazi).
  • Schindler said Gemini is going into “our entire ads infrastructure”; over 30% of Search ad spend now runs through AI campaigns.
  • Google will “begin to deliver TPUs to a select group of customers in their own data centers” (TPUs are Google’s own AI chips). Most of that revenue lands in 2027.
  • 2026 capital spending guidance rose to $180–190B, and 2027 is expected to “significantly increase.” Quarterly free cash flow (cash left after running the business and investing) was $10.1B.
  • Waymo passed “500,000 fully autonomous rides per week,” doubling in under a year. YouTube Premium had its biggest quarterly subscriber gain since 2018.
Where analysts pushed
  • Doug Anmuth (JP Morgan) on whether spending can keep up with the backlog. Ashkenazi gave no 2027 number: “we’ll provide more clarity in a future earnings call.”
  • Michael Nathanson (MoffettNathanson) on who gets scarce computing power. Pichai: model training comes first, and “our Cloud revenue would have been higher if we were able to meet the demand.”
  • Nathanson on ads in the Gemini app. Schindler: AI Mode formats “would transfer successfully to Gemini app,” but “we’re not rushing anything here.”
  • Mark Shmulik (Bernstein) on how much changed user behavior drives Search. Schindler didn’t size it, calling it “the expansionary moment we see here for Search.”
  • Ross Sandler (Barclays) on ad prices as shopping agents spread. Schindler stayed on user experience: “all else will follow.”
  • Justin Post (BofA) on margins on the biggest AI deals. Pichai: “nothing to comment on any specific contracts.”

Q2 2026 · Jul 22

What management highlighted
  • Search revenue rose 17%. AI Mode “surpassed one billion monthly active users” and is “driving an incremental increase in Search queries overall.” The Gemini app has 950 million monthly users (Pichai).
  • Cloud revenue rose 82% to $24.8B, the backlog reached $514B, and operating margin was 35.6%. The first TPU system sales were booked, and growth “accelerated meaningfully, even after excluding” them (Ashkenazi).
  • Advertisers using AI Max or PMax (Google’s automated campaigns) “see an average of 15% more conversions or value on Search at a similar ROAS” (return on ad spend), per Schindler.
  • YouTube ads grew 13%; over 1.7 billion people watched World Cup videos, “the most‑viewed World Cup in YouTube history” (Schindler).
  • 2026 spending guidance rose again, to $195–205B. Free cash flow was “negative... $5.9 billion” and is expected to “remain under pressure” (Ashkenazi).
  • In Q3, Search will “begin lapping an acceleration... that began in the third quarter last year” (a tougher comparison). Ashkenazi also flagged “modest margin pressure” in Cloud from renting outside capacity.
Where analysts pushed
  • Doug Anmuth (JP Morgan) on whether Gemini can stay in front. Pichai: “there are areas where we have acknowledged we need to improve. Coding and agentic coding is an example,” pointing to Gemini 4.
  • Ross Sandler (Barclays) on release speed. Pichai: “releasing models almost at a monthly cadence.”
  • Anmuth on funding. Ashkenazi: debt rose from about $16B to about $100B in a year, and “we’re not planning to go back to the equity markets,” apart from a program covering taxes on stock pay.
  • Nathanson (MoffettNathanson) and Eric Sheridan (Goldman Sachs) on TPU margins. Ashkenazi: “we don’t break out margins for any specific products.”
  • Mark Shmulik (Bernstein) on paying up for outside capacity. Pichai: the short-term cost “may be very high,” but the deals are “highly ROI‑positive” over their life.
  • Ken Gawrelski (Wells Fargo) on spinning out Waymo. Pichai: “We’re really focused on scaling the business right now.”

What to watch next (Oct 28): Whether free cash flow stays negative, how Search does against the tougher comparison, and any timing for ads in the Gemini app.

Booking Holdings BKNG

Speakers: Glenn Fogel (CEO), Ewout Steenbergen (CFO). Neither call had real pushback.

Q1 2026 · Apr 28

What management highlighted
  • The Middle East conflict hit travel. Room nights (nights booked) rose 6%; Fogel said they “would’ve been up by approximately 8%” without it. March was only +1%.
  • Q2 guidance: room nights +2–4%; gross bookings (total value of travel booked), revenue and adjusted EBITDA (a profit measure before interest, tax and non-cash items) +4–6%. Full-year midpoints lowered; long-term targets unchanged (at least 8% bookings and revenue growth, 15% adjusted EPS growth).
  • US room nights “accelerated for the fourth consecutive quarter to the low teens.” Trips with more than one product grew in the high teens; Genius loyalty levels 2–3 are a “high-50% share of room nights.”
  • AI: Priceline’s assistant Penny showed “noticeable uplift”; Agoda cut service cost per booking by double digits; partners include “OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and Amazon.”
  • A record “$3.6 billion in share repurchases.”
Where analysts pushed
  • Kopelman (TD Cowen) on the conflict’s hit to Q2: about 3 points, “Anyone can put whatever you want in your model”; Fogel: “we do know it will end.”
  • Post (BofA) on AI as a threat: “an absolute positive for us, not a negative.”
  • Mahaney (Evercore) on airfares and gas prices: “too early to draw any conclusions... not completely out of the woods.”
  • Nowak (Morgan Stanley) on limits for AI booking agents: “definitely not a compute problem”; the constraints are AI rules, privacy and payments, especially in Europe.

Q2 2026 · Aug 4

What management highlighted
  • Beat the top of guidance: room nights +5%, gross bookings +9%, revenue +8%, adjusted EBITDA about $2.6B (+9%), adjusted EPS $2.54 (+15%).
  • Domestic room nights grew high single digits; international “increased slightly” (long-haul flights). The US slowed to high single digits.
  • Merchant bookings (where Booking takes payment itself) were about 73% of gross bookings, up 4 points.
  • Q3 guidance: room nights +3–5%, the other measures +4–6%. Full-year bookings lowered because of flights; accommodation “largely unchanged.”
  • Cost-savings target raised from about $550M to $650M. First-half buybacks were $7.4B at about $173 a share. Voice AI handles “the majority of eligible inbound traveler calls.”
Where analysts pushed
  • Mahaney on proof that AI lifts bookings: “still very small... not... giving away percentages”; Steenbergen: AI chatbot traffic is “significantly below 1% of our room nights.”
  • Post on flights and rental cars: airline capacity and fares weighed; Booking’s tickets +4% still beat the industry; accommodation is “the biggest part of our economics.”
  • Walmsley (Mizuho) on falling search traffic and hotels selling direct: Google AI Overviews came up; Booking’s direct share held in the mid-60s %; on hotels, “I don’t know, they don’t tell me.”
  • Kopelman on the conflict hit: “a bit higher than... the first quarter,” normalizing in June.
  • Ju (UBS) on AI returns: “I don’t know what they’re doing” (aimed at doubters); AI is a “low single digit” share of tech spending.

What to watch next (Nov 3): Whether travel demand normalizes after the conflict, and any harder evidence that AI lifts bookings.

Interactive Brokers IBKR

Speakers: Nancy Stuebe (IR, reading the CEO’s prepared remarks), Paul Brody (CFO), Milan Galik (CEO), Thomas Peterffy (founder and chairman).

Q1 2026 · Apr 21

What management highlighted
  • Stuebe: “We continued to set records across key metrics, including net revenue, total accounts, and account adds.” Client equity (assets clients hold at IBKR) rose 38% to $789B while the S&P 500 fell 5%.
  • “Our pre-tax profit margin was 77%... the sixth consecutive quarter with margins above 70%.” The dividend rose from $0.32 to $0.35 a year.
  • Brody: commissions up 19%, “reaching over $600 million for the first time”; net interest income (earned on client cash and loans) up 17% to $904M.
  • Rate sensitivity: a quarter-point Fed cut means “an $82 million reduction in annual net interest income,” plus about $35M for a quarter-point cut outside the US.
  • New products: crypto in Europe, Coinbase perpetual futures, prediction markets “trading 24/7”; overnight trades “nearly tripled” to 8.1M.
Where analysts pushed
  • Goldman on marketing: Peterffy, “hell-bent on trying to increase our marketing spend, but... very strict about getting the required minimum return”; the increase “is going very slowly.”
  • Goldman on AI tools that push brokers to pay more on cash: “We’re not happy about these tools because we have always been paying close to market rates.”
  • Barclays on April margin loans (loans to clients against their holdings): only the quarter-end figure, “$86.6 billion.”
  • BMO on non-US rates by currency: “we don’t really get into it at that granular level.”
  • KBW on crypto gaps: Galik, “We are not covering all the geographies”; staking depends on partner Zero Hash.

Q2 2026 · Jul 21

What management highlighted
  • Records in “commissions, net interest, and total net revenue... total accounts, account adds, client equity, and total client DARTs” (daily average revenue trades). Client equity rose 40% to $930B; new accounts +34%.
  • A 77% margin, “the seventh consecutive quarter with margins above 70%.” Commissions “up 30%... to a new record”; net interest income +23% “to over $1 billion.”
  • A quarter-point Fed move is now worth about $81M a year; IBKR is “pushing out our duration a bit” (locking in rates for longer).
  • New: “the first e-broker to offer trading in Korea”; SpaceX IPO shares offered to UK and European retail; IBKR Connector links clients’ AI chatbots to their accounts; one place for prediction markets (ForecastEx, CME, Kalshi).
  • Preliminary conditional OCC approval for a national trust bank charter, “operational by year-end.”
Where analysts pushed
  • Wolfe on marketing returns: Peterffy, “I do not think that the marketing yield has in fact been higher than it was before”; on 20%+ growth, “we don’t like to promise.”
  • Wolfe on margin loans: “we are very cognizant of our client risk on margins... and we feel comfortable with the current levels.”
  • Goldman on spare capital: about “$10.3 billion”; “Nothing so far stood out as worthy pursuing.”
  • Goldman on China’s crackdown on Tiger and Futu: “We have been in compliance with the Chinese mainland regulations for a long time”; “a clear uptick” in transfers.
  • Piper Sandler on trading per account: “partially due to the strong environment.”
  • BMO on fully autonomous AI trading: clients still approve every AI-drafted order; fully autonomous “at some point,” with guardrails.

What to watch next (Oct 15): Whether account growth and the 70%+ margin streak hold, and how rate moves hit interest income.

Spotify SPOT

Speakers: Christian Luiga (CFO), Alex Norström and Gustav Söderström (co-CEOs).

Q1 2026 · Apr 28

What management highlighted
  • “MAU grew by 10 million to 761 million in total, surpassing our guidance by 2 million” (Luiga). MAU = monthly active users. Users “are now listening and watching more days per month” (Norström).
  • Subscribers rose 3M to 293M, with “no surprises with respect to price increase related churn” after January’s US price rise.
  • Revenue EUR 4.5B (+14%); gross margin 33%, “our second-highest gross margin ever.”
  • Operating margin 15.8%; the beat came mostly from lower social charges (payroll tax on stock pay): “excluding the non-forecasted social charge favorability, we came in approximately EUR 6 million above guidance.”
  • “we expect operating expenses to remain at these levels for the next quarter or two”; Q2 operating income guided to EUR 630M.
Where analysts pushed
  • Greenfield on ads: “Why is increased engagement not translating to accelerating ad revenue growth?” Norström: “it’s about execution, it’s about patience”; Luiga sees a pickup in the second half.
  • Reif Ehrlich and Sheridan on AI and spending: Söderström, “we have not increased our headcount... We are spending more compute per employee”; Luiga, “the operating margin will improve year-over-year.”
  • Black on flat gross margin: “variability.”
  • Patterson on free-tier costs: engagement “is driving more content costs right now than the income on top line,” “a short-term issue.”
  • Packer on AI disruption: taste “is not easily commoditized because it’s not a fact, it’s an opinion.”

Q2 2026 · Aug 4

What management highlighted
  • Subscribers passed 300M for the first time; revenue +15%. “Gross margin hit a record of 33.4%.”
  • First miss on users: “Our net additions of 16 million were 1 million below forecast”; still, “active days in a month has increased overall for Premium subscribers.”
  • A deliberate free-tier slowdown in emerging markets: Spotify “carefully increases friction in our free service... This will show itself in our Q3 MAU.” Q3 guide: +11M to 788M.
  • Operating margin 13.7% (down from 15.8%); operating income EUR 655M, EUR 25M above guidance. Q3 guide EUR 670M on about EUR 5B revenue (about 13.4% by the desk’s math).
  • Costs “temporarily elevated... in quarter two and quarter three,” with “a moderation” in Q4.
  • Automated ad channels are “nearly 40% of ad-supported revenue” and “active advertisers grew 60%,” yet ad revenue grew only 3%.
Where analysts pushed
  • Reif Ehrlich on weak ads: the ad-tech transition is done; Luiga, “expect our ads business to inflect towards double-digit growth in the second half of 2026.”
  • Levi on the slower user guide: “here we’re starting to pull the monetization lever.”
  • Anmuth on Q3 costs: “roughly consistent with quarter two”; Söderström, “We are sticking to our cost moderating in Q4.”
  • Bazinet on industry subscriber growth: “I’m very confident. We don’t give guidance for long-term.”
  • Greenfield and Bazinet on AI music deals: “we do not need a deal with all the majors”; a research preview promised, no date.

What to watch next (Oct 22): Operating margin fell in Q2 and the Q3 guide points to a second dip. The desk says no falsifier has been hit yet, but a second quarter-on-quarter decline would start to look like the sustained drop it watches for.

Uber UBER

Speakers: Dara Khosrowshahi (CEO) and Krishnamurthy (CFO, as named by the desk).

Q1 2026 · May 6

What management highlighted
  • Gross bookings (total value of rides and orders) rose 21%. Khosrowshahi: “we delivered top line and profitability at or above the high end of our guidance.”
  • Mobility +20% “with record margins,” Delivery +23%; “Freight returned to growth for the first time in nearly two years.”
  • “Non-GAAP EPS increased 44% year over year”; “a record $3 billion” of buybacks.
  • Uber “surpassed 50 million Uber One members and 10 million drivers and couriers globally.”
  • “AV Mobility trips grew more than 10x year-on-year” (AV = autonomous vehicles), live “in up to 15 cities by the end of the year.”
  • Krishnamurthy: “the first year since COVID” with “good leverage on our insurance cost line” in US Mobility.
Where analysts pushed
  • Mostly friendly. Post (BofA) on Waymo: “we don’t see any effect of the Waymo launches on our overall business.”
  • Morton (MoffettNathanson) on AI agents: “we’ve seen this movie before”; “the majority of those experiences are going to come direct to us.”
  • Colantuoni (Jefferies) on AI spending: “we underestimated the amount of impact the AI tools could have,” offset by “slower head count growth.”
  • Devnani (Bernstein) on the payoff of investing at lower margins: there “isn’t a single formula.”
  • Josey (Citi) on AV limits: “we just need more cars on the road,” “this is going to take time.”

Q2 2026 · Aug 5

What management highlighted
  • Gross bookings rose 22% to “more than $58 billion,” the “fourth consecutive quarter above 20% growth”; non-GAAP EPS +35%.
  • “trailing 12-month free cash flow exceeding $10 billion for the first time.”
  • Delivery Hero takes Uber “to nearly 100 markets”; the main tech migration is in 2029, assuming the deal closes in the second half of 2027.
  • AV: “the world’s leading commercialization platform for autonomous vehicles,” live in 7 cities, 15 by year-end.
  • On the US rides speed-up: “all in, it’s broad and sustainable.”
Where analysts pushed
  • Anmuth (JPM) on Brazil and Waymo: in the delivery fight “the cost of securing that supply has gone up pretty significantly,” but share held; “we want to make sure that we’re not dependent on one partner.”
  • Post on revenue (+19%) lagging bookings (+22%): about 4 of the roughly 5 points come from a UK accounting change, “an optical impact”; on $10B of AV spending, “we’ll size that for investors clearly.”
  • Sheridan (Goldman) on the World Cup: it “was a benefit,” but growth “is far broader than any one-off event.”
  • Mahaney (Evercore) on buybacks after about $4B into Delivery Hero stock: “months, not quarters.”
  • Devnani on integration: “very high confidence on our integration plan.”
  • Colantuoni on AV regulation: “sometimes you got to slow down to drive sustainable regulation.”

What to watch next (Nov 4): Whether bookings stay above 20%, when buybacks restart, and how the AV rollout tracks toward 15 cities.

Zillow Z

Speakers: Jeremy Wacksman (CEO), Jeremy Hofmann (CFO).

Q1 2026 · May 6

What management highlighted
  • Revenue +18% to $708M, EBITDA $182M, net income $46M, in what Wacksman called “a housing market that was essentially flat.” For Sale +12%, mortgages +56%, rentals +42%, multifamily +57%.
  • Zillow Home Loans purchase volume +96% to a record $1.5B; now a “top 25 purchase lender.”
  • Enhanced markets were 49% of connections (buyer leads sent to agents), against a target of at least 75%.
  • AI Mode is live for about 5% of users: “Early signals are encouraging.” Zillow Preview grew from 5 to more than 60 brokerages in 7 weeks; a realtor.com partnership was announced the day before.
  • $626M of buybacks. Hofmann expects margins to widen in the second half as FTC-trial legal costs ease, rentals investment slows and ad spending normalizes.
Where analysts pushed
  • McKeveny (Zelman) on how much of the margin ramp Zillow controls: Hofmann, “a lot is in our control.”
  • Erickson (RBC) on residential starting “a little softer”: “we’re planning for the housing market to continue to be effectively flat”; on mortgages, “we’re not yet at scale. Margins are improving.”
  • Josey (Citi) on rentals: the full-year 30% guide implies “upper 20%” growth in the second half.
  • Colantuoni (Jefferies) on the Q2 residential slowdown: “MVP lags a bit because of that agent sentiment.”
  • Devnani (Bernstein) on the mid-teens growth target: “well intact for mid-teens.”

Q2 2026 · Aug 5

What management highlighted
  • Revenue +18% to $772M, EBITDA $176M, adjusted net income $118M; mortgages +75%, rentals +31%. Q3 guidance is about +11%, with residential flat.
  • A faster move to Preferred (agents pay Zillow at closing instead of up front): more than 75% of connections by end-2026, from 61% now. Revenue per connection was 23% above the old model in 2025, with a 35% target by end-2026.
  • That shifts revenue from residential to mortgages by 6–7 points in Q3 and 7–8 in Q4, and mortgage revenue is booked 6–12 months after the connection. Hofmann: “The integrated transaction strategy is working.”
  • About 7% of roles cut, for $75M of annual savings. Hofmann became COO as well as CFO; Jun Choo stepped down as COO; a chief legal and policy officer was hired from Google.
  • AI Mode is live for about 20% of signed-in users, who “contact an agent at nearly 3x the rate.”
Where analysts pushed
  • McKeveny on the leadership change: Wacksman, “No change in strategy”; Hofmann, “we do not expect any impact to our growth bets.”
  • Kurnos (StoneX) on Google’s real-estate push and litigation: “not seeing any impact”; about 80% of traffic is direct; litigation is “not impacting our business.”
  • Erickson on whether Preferred is “dilutive”: home-loan unit economics are now profitable, with “a path to dollars per loan at the same level as preferred agent referral fees.”
  • Josey on Showcase at 5% of new listings (“I would have thought maybe we would have seen greater penetration”): up from 2.5% a year ago, above 10% in the top 10 markets, “We are nowhere near done.”
  • Devnani on residential going from mid-single-digit growth to flat: slower housing, purchase mortgages now seen down low-to-mid single digits, plus the faster Preferred shift.
  • Walmsley (Mizuho) on what drives the revenue-per-connection uplift: no split given.

What to watch next (Nov 4): Whether residential is flat as guided and the Preferred shift shows up in mortgage revenue.
