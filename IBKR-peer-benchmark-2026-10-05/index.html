Interactive Brokers — peer benchmark

IBKR-peer-benchmark-2026-10-05 · USD · series 2026-10-05T18:00:00Z · built 2026-10-05 17:54 UTC

The short version

Customers pay Interactive Brokers when they trade and when they keep cash and securities on the platform. The firm takes a commission on the trade, smaller fees for market data and services, and net interest on the balances it carries. Schwab, Fidelity, and Robinhood do the same job for retail investors who trade and leave cash. Charles Schwab is the large U.S. retail broker. Fidelity is a private firm in the same retail brokerage and cash business. Robinhood is an app broker, with more payment for order flow and interest and less of a desk for professional orders.

Schwab is the larger revenue base. Interactive Brokers and Robinhood sit closer to each other in dollars.

Pretax is the profit line these brokers print. Interactive Brokers converts more of each revenue dollar than Schwab or Robinhood on the overlapping prints. Robinhood’s pretax margin is derived.

Open gaps

  • Fidelity does not print isolated brokerage net revenues, pretax, margin, or a traded PE — it stays blank on those charts.
  • Schwab annual net revenues and pretax before FY2012 are not on this page.
  • Robinhood’s public annual history starts in FY2021; quarters before FY2022Q1 are blank.
  • Schwab and Robinhood have no house weekly PE series here.
  • Commissions, Schwab trading revenue, and Robinhood transaction-based revenue are not one unit price.
  • DARTs, DATs, and Robinhood notional are not one activity unit.
