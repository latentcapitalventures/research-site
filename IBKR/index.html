Interactive Brokers closed Q2’26 with 5.185 million accounts, $930.3B of customer equity, and 4.824M daily average revenue trades at $2.64 of commission per cleared order. Commissions were $673M and other fees $87M — together about 40% of $1,896M net revenues — at a ~76.8% pretax margin. The stock trades at 34.67× trailing earnings; the street’s forward prints sit at 33.52× (FY1) and 28.46× (FY2).

The long case here is a fee toll, an API moat, and globality. This pack measures the toll and the globe. The API pillar is still unmeasured — a gap, not a chart. Net interest income is a one-line archive footnote, not the hero.

The tollbooth — commissions, with other fees growing faster

Commissions are the toll. Q2’26 printed $673M, up 30.4% on a year ago and up from $613M in Q1. The line has compounded from roughly $90M a quarter in 2010 to nearly seven times that, through zero-commission price wars and a meme-stock volume boom, without the desk ever having to invent a number.

Other fees and services printed $87M, up 40.3% on a year ago — still a much smaller absolute line than commissions, but growing faster. Together the two fee lines are about 40% of net revenues. That is a mix share, not a margin claim; interest and other net-revenue lines exist without needing to be the story.

Inside the disclosed fee stack, commissions are ~89%

The contract-revenue note breaks the fee stack into types. In Q2’26: commissions $673M, market data $23M, risk exposure $25M, payment for order flow $18M, FDIC sweep $10M, other $11M. Commissions are about 89% of the disclosed stack, and the stack itself only exists as a quarterly disclosure from early 2024 — that is when this chart starts.

The non-commission lines sum to $87M — the same dollars as the P&L’s other-fees line this quarter. That is a fact check on this print, not a universal identity. Two things the note cannot do: minimum-activity fees are null across the pack and stay null, and no API or Lite attach rate can be derived from order-flow or sweep lines — those are fee types, not product telemetry.

Scale — accounts and equity keep compounding

Accounts reached 5.185M at the end of Q2, up 34.1% on a year ago and 9.1% on the prior quarter. Customer equity reached $930.3B, up 40.0% year over year. Both series only become dense around 2020 in this extract; earlier quarters stay empty rather than backfilled.

Activity — more trades, a steady toll per trade

DARTs printed 4.824M, up 35.8% on a year ago. The commission per cleared commissionable order was $2.64, versus $2.65 a year ago and $3.16 when the dense series begins in early 2023. That is a gentle glide, not a collapse of the toll: activity scaled up while the take per order held in the mid-$2s.

P&L shape — a high-margin fee engine

Net revenues were $1,896M, up 28.1% on a year ago; pretax income was $1,456M, up 31.9%. A ~76.8% pretax margin, versus ~74.6% a year ago, is what a tollbooth P&L looks like. The fee lines above are the hero; the rest of net revenues is visible in the total but not broken out in this pack’s income series.

Archive footnote: net interest income, NIM, and interest-earning balances are disclosed in the MD&A but have no analytics table this cut, and market-making segment history is legacy. They stay archive-only here — noted, not charted, and not promoted into the fee-toll thesis.

Globality — international is about a third of the house

The management geo split puts US net revenues at $1,284M and international at $612M in Q2’26 — international is about 32.3% of the two combined, with pretax income of $1,076M US and $380M international. A third of the house outside the US is supportive of the globality pillar, not a stall print.

This is the management geo P&L — a different object from the contract-revenue location in the ASC 606 note (US $457M / Intl $303M this quarter). The two are footnoted, never blended.

Method & definitional notes
  • ASC 606 contract revenue by location (US/Intl) and management geo P&L (US/Intl net revenues, pretax) are different disclosure objects — never blended on one chart.
  • pe_ttm prints only where trailing-four-quarter net income is complete and positive; sparse markers, never connected or interpolated. pe_fy is FY-end only — null on quarterly rows, and pe_ttm is null on FY rows.
  • The FY2025Q4 net-income leg inside TTM is a residual (FY − Q1−Q3) and is labeled as such wherever TTM construction is shown.
  • Weekly trailing and official forward PE use the latest complete ISO week (2026-W37); price is a volume-weighted average of daily closes, not a true trade-time VWAP. USD/USD — no FX translation.
  • Forward PE is the single official W37 print; pre-snapshot history is null by design, and NTM is null until the four-quarter estimate stack is complete. Trailing and forward sit side by side — never blended into one series.
  • ASC 606 min_activity is null across the pack — left null, not invented. No API or Lite attach rate is derived from PFOF or FDIC sweep fee types.
  • Net interest income / NIM / market-making are archive-only for this pack (no analytics table this cut) — a footnote, not a hero series.
  • Pre-2015 MD&A-era net-revenue / pretax stubs are unit-noisy; trajectory charts use the dense modern window and missing quarters stay empty.
  • Mid-2025 share split: diluted WAS steps ~110M → ~441M between FY2025Q1 and FY2025Q2; pre/post-split price levels are not continuous.
  • Recent quarterly net income is sparse in the pack income slice (non-null 2010–2013 only); TTM / FY net-income legs come from the valuation extract, with the residual Q4 labeled.
  • Non-additive: ASC 606 contract revenue by location and management geo P&L are different objects — never blended.
  • Non-additive: Commissions appear in both the P&L fee-toll panel and the ASC 606 stack — same dollars, different disclosure objects; do not sum across panels.
