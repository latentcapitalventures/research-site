Zillow — key metrics, Q2’26

Z · USD · series 2026-09-20T15:05:00Z · built 2026-09-20 15:00 UTC

The short version

Zillow’s Q2’26 reads best through two lines: how hard the marketplace monetizes each dollar of industry transaction value, and how fast connections move onto success-fee terms. For Sale revenue per industry TTV printed 8.8 bps TTM — ticking up from 8.7 in Q1 but still below the ~10.1–10.3 that held through FY24–3Q25, a step-down that overlaps the TTV source flip to NAR. Preferred / Enhanced connections reached 49% by Q1’26, up from 21% at the end of 2024, with management guiding past 75% by end-2026. Around that spine: revenue of $772M with $176M of Adj. EBITDA, Rentals up ~31% and Zillow Home Loans origination nearly doubled, an audience roughly flat at 2,529M visits and 239M AMUU, and a stock at roughly 130× trailing earnings on a thin, just-turned-positive earnings base.

Long-only, 5–10 year horizon: the market overweights agentic-AI disruption versus Zillow’s marketplace and data moat. The primary falsifier is sustained audience decay; the secondary is AI-search taking a fast-rising share of housing queries. But the unit of product is not the audience — it is the bps of industry transaction value Zillow captures, and the attachment mix that should expand it. That is why those two lines lead this page; the audience, Comscore reach, Rentals and ZHL are the support cast.

The spine — monetization intensity × attachment

For Sale revenue per industry total transaction value, trailing twelve months, ran 10.1–10.3 bps from FY2024 through 3Q25 — then printed 8.5 at FY2025, 8.7 in 1Q26 and 8.8 in 2Q26. Read the level reset with care: beginning with the fourth quarter of 2025 the TTV denominator comes from NAR-published home counts and prices instead of Zillow’s own estimates, and recasting the TTM ended June 2025 onto NAR moved TTV up roughly 26% and the bps ratio down roughly 20%. The dotted marker on the chart is that methodology boundary; the live question is whether the slope rebuilds from the high-8s, and the last two prints do tick up.

The mechanism that is supposed to rebuild it sits in the right panel: moving connections onto success-fee terms. A connection is a validated lead — consumer contact information delivered to a Premier Agent partner — not a closing; closing is what triggers the Preferred fee. Preferred / Enhanced connections were 21% of connections at the end of 2024 and reached 49% by Q1’26, through 24, 27, 34 and 44 at the quarter-ends in between. The issuer’s label shifted across those prints (Enhanced Markets vs Enhanced Market experience), and the 2Q26 letter restates the 2024 and 2025 year-ends as “connections in Preferred” at the same 21% / 44% levels while guiding past 75% by end-2026 — the series is one period-end % of connections throughout, era labels kept. The falsifier link is explicit: if attachment keeps rising and bps do not follow, the mix shift is not pricing power.

A newer, separate metric — integrated experience via Preferred partners — printed 61% of connections at Q2’26. It is a different definition from the Enhanced Markets attachment series, labeled here only so the two are not conflated; it is never merged into the line above.

Revenue by engine — For Sale vs Rentals

The engine stack splits as-filed quarters into Residential, Mortgages and Rentals. Residential plus Mortgages is exactly the issuer’s For Sale grouping — printed as one For Sale line from FY2024 year-end — so the stack reads identically across that disclosure boundary: $465M + $84M = $549M of For Sale in Q2’26, against $209M of Rentals. Full-year anchors: FY2025 For Sale $1,903M and Rentals $630M, versus FY2024 $1,739M and $453M.

The Q4 quarters are marked with a baseline ×, not bars: Q4’23, Q4’24 and Q4’25 exist in the file only as FY − Q1−Q3 residuals for revenue and net income, and the residual path carries no segment split. Nothing is invented for those slots.

Second engines — Rentals and ZHL

Rentals revenue reached $209M in Q2’26 versus $159M a year ago, about +31% — the clearest growth line in the stack. Zillow Home Loans purchase origination hit $2.17B versus $1.12B, nearly doubling year over year. Together they are the answer to a flat audience: more transaction per visit, not more visits. They support the spine rather than lead it — Rentals is still ~27% of quarterly revenue, and ZHL feeds the same attachment thesis.

Audience — flat, not broken

Demoted from the lead, and deliberately so: the audience is a falsifier gauge, not the unit of value. Q2’26 visits of 2,529M and 239M average monthly unique users sit just below Q2’25 (2,590M / 243M) — a low-single-digit drift, not a collapse. Q1 was softer at 2,276M / 220M against 2,354M / 227M a year earlier. Two years of quarterly prints show a plateau, not a downtrend break — exactly what the primary falsifier watches.

Full-year visit totals are annual sums (FY2025: 9,593M) and are labeled as such in the file; they never share an axis with the quarterly series. Visits and AMUU are issuer-counted engagement measures — not Comscore MUV.

Definitions

TTV — Total Transaction Value
“TTV is calculated as the number of existing residential homes sold during the relevant period multiplied by the average sales price of existing residential homes sold during the same period.” It is Total Transaction Value for the residential real estate industry — not Zillow’s own closed GMV, and there is no separate company-TTV Key Metric. Source change: before the three months ended Dec 31, 2025 Zillow collected/estimated existing-home sales and ASP; beginning with that quarter the inputs are NAR-published. The TTM ended Jun 30, 2025 recast to NAR was an approximately 26% increase in TTV and a 20% decrease in For Sale revenue per TTV versus the prior Zillow-estimated basis. Q2 2026 Form 10-Q MD&A Key Metrics, accession 0001617640-26-000052
For Sale revenue / TTV (TTM bps)
“We calculate For Sale revenue per TTV as total For Sale revenue for the relevant period divided by the aggregate TTV for the same period.” Trailing-twelve-month basis, in basis points. Cabinet series: 10.1 (FY2024) → 10.2 / 10.3 / 10.1 (1Q–3Q25) → 8.5 (FY2025) → 8.7 (1Q26) → 8.8 (2Q26) — the step into the high-8s overlaps the NAR methodology window, so the slope across 4Q25 straddles two TTV sources. Q2 2026 10-Q (0001617640-26-000052); metric dictionary for_sale_rev_per_ttv_bps_ttm
Connections
“Connections are delivered when consumer contact information is provided to Premier Agent partners.” A connection is a validated lead — not a closing. Under Zillow Preferred, partners “pay a performance advertising fee when a real estate transaction is closed with one of the leads, generally within two years”: closing triggers the fee, not the connection count. Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, accession 0001617640-26-000052
Zillow Preferred vs Enhanced Markets
Preferred is the pay-for-performance Premier Agent pricing product; Enhanced Markets are the geographies “where the integrated housing super app experience is most fully realized” (4Q24 letter). The % of connections series prints 21 (Q4’24) → 24 → 27 → 34 → 44 (Q4’25) → 49 (Q1’26) under era labels (“Enhanced Markets” / “Enhanced Market experience”). The 2Q26 letter restates YE2024 / YE2025 as “connections in Preferred” at 21% / 44% — the same levels — and guides to more than 75% of connections in Preferred by end of 2026. Preferred is the product umbrella for the attachment goal; Enhanced Markets remains the geo / experience framing. Shareholder letters 0001617640-25-000015 (4Q24), 0001617640-26-000012 (4Q25), 0001617640-26-000038 (1Q26); desk dig z-preferred-enhanced-dig-2026-09-17
Integrated experience % of connections (61%)
“…this integrated experience through our Preferred partners, and it now accounts for 61% of our connections across Zillow.” A separate field (integrated_experience_pct_connections, 61 at 2026-06-30 only). The issuer did not put 61% into the Enhanced Markets / “connections in Preferred” series table, so the two are never merged unless an issuer table equates them. 2Q26 letter 0001617640-26-000050 Ex99.2
Visits
A group of interactions on the Zillow, Trulia, and StreetEasy apps and sites. Trulia visits end after 30 minutes of inactivity; Zillow and StreetEasy visits end after 30 minutes of inactivity or at midnight. Measured with an internal tool for Zillow / StreetEasy and Adobe Analytics for Trulia. FY rows store annual visit totals (FY2025: 9,593M) and are never mixed with the quarterly series. Q2 2026 10-Q Key Metrics, accession 0001617640-26-000052
AMUU — average monthly unique users
Unique users are counted as the first access per mobile app / web browser / device / website in a calendar month — one consumer on two browsers counts twice. AMUU averages that monthly count across the period. Issuer count rules; not a Comscore measure. Q2 2026 10-Q Key Metrics, accession 0001617640-26-000052
Comscore audience share (issuer-quoted)
Zillow Group average monthly unique visitors ÷ Comscore “real estate” category unique visitors, as quoted in issuer materials: 70% at both 2026-03-31 and 2026-06-30, with group Comscore MUV of 127M cited once at 1Q26.
