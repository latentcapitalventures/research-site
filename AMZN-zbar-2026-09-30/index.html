Letter open

Amazon runs retail and marketplace (own sites and third-party sellers), advertising, subscriptions (including Prime), and cloud (AWS) under one roof. Filings report three segments with their own operating income — North America, International, and AWS. Product-line cuts (Online stores, Third-party seller services, Advertising, Subscriptions) mostly appear as net-sales slices, not separate profit lines; AWS is both a product line and a reportable segment.

Growth is still led by AWS and advertising inside the retail shell; North America and International carry the retail, ads, and subscription stack by geography. This pack locks consolidated P&L, issuer-definition free cash flow, segment operating income where printed, and trailing weekly PE. Forward PE stays a labeled gap until a locked NTM print exists. No long thesis yet.

Major risks (≤4)

  • Capex cycle can erase free cash flow even when operating income is rising (issuer FCF deep negative in H1’26).
  • Retail and marketplace competition and mix shift can pressure North America / International margins without showing up as separate product-line profit lines.
  • AWS growth and margin are the main profit engine; a durable slowdown there changes the consolidated story fast.
  • Large one-period net-income moves (e.g. Q2’26) can dominate GAAP without a locked cause in this pack — do not invent one.

Close comps (≤5)

  • Microsoft / Google Cloud — hyperscale cloud peers for AWS.
  • Walmart / Target — big-box retail and marketplace pressure in North America.
  • Shopify / eBay — third-party marketplace and seller-services overlap.
  • Meta / Google ads — advertising demand competing for the same dollars.
  • Costco — membership / retail adjacency for Prime-style subscription economics.
