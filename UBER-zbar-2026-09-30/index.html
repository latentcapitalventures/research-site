Intro

Uber runs a technology marketplace that matches riders with drivers, local orders with couriers, and shippers with carriers — three reportable lines: Mobility, Delivery, and Freight, across more than seventy countries. Drivers and couriers earn on the platform; Uber’s GAAP Revenue is the platform cut (fees, commissions, and related). Gross Bookings is the issuer’s scale print (dollar value of rides, orders, and Freight under its definition); Revenue is a slice of that, which the issuer names Revenue Margin. Do not treat Revenue and Gross Bookings as interchangeable.

Profitability on this pack is GAAP income from operations and free cash flow (operating cash flow minus purchases of property and equipment — the issuer’s FCF identity). Net income and diluted EPS can swing with equity-method and other investment revaluations without matching the operating-income path — read the bottom line with that noise. Gross Bookings, Trips, Monthly Active Platform Consumers, Revenue Margin, and segment cuts stay on the unit-economics dig until Product harvests those series for a second pass.

Risks

  • Equity-investment revaluations can dominate GAAP net income / EPS in a single quarter without matching operating income or cash.
  • Regulatory, classification, and insurance outcomes for drivers and couriers can reprice the platform cut or cost stack.
  • Competitive intensity in rides and local delivery can pressure Gross Bookings growth or Revenue Margin.
  • Freight remains a smaller, more cyclical brokerage line than Mobility and Delivery.

Close comps

  • Lyft — U.S.-weighted rides marketplace; closest Mobility peer on bookings and take.
  • DoorDash — local commerce delivery scale; Delivery comps on Gross Bookings and margin.
  • Amazon (local commerce / logistics adjacency) — Delivery competitive pressure, not a like-for-like rides peer.
  • Public freight brokers (e.g. CHRW) — Freight brokerage comps only; not the Mobility/Delivery story.
