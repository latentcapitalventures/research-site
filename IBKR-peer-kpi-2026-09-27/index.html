Dig Q

Across the latest overlapping quarterly prints through Q2’26, how do IBKR’s fee intensity, client scale, and pretax economics compare to Schwab and Robinhood on a common KPI footing — and where do definition mismatches block a fair scoreboard?

Short verdict

Mixed. On a shared net-revenue and pretax footing through Q2’26, IBKR remains the pretax-margin standout (~77% vs Schwab’s GAAP ~52% and Robinhood’s derived ~54%). Scale still belongs to Schwab. Fee intensity and activity do not clear a fair three-way scoreboard. The prints below are the latest overlapping quarter, ended 2026-06-30.

Common footing: revenues and pretax

The comparable P&L spine is net revenues and pretax. Q2’26: IBKR net revenues $1.896B, Schwab $7.072B, Robinhood $1.308B. Pretax income: IBKR $1.456B, Schwab $3.669B, Robinhood $709M. Margins: IBKR 76.8%, Schwab GAAP pre-tax 51.9%, Robinhood ~54.2% DERIVED (pretax divided by net revenues — Robinhood headlines net income and adjusted EBITDA more than a GAAP pretax margin).

That is the honesty panel. IBKR’s tollbooth economics still show up as operating leverage and pretax conversion that neither public peer matches on this cut — even though Schwab’s absolute pretax dollars are larger on a much larger revenue base. Interactive Brokers has no December-quarter row in the quarterly series this page copies. That quarter stays blank.

Fee dollars grew at all three. IBKR commissions $673M. Schwab trading revenue $1.215B. Robinhood transaction-based revenue $776M, with event contracts a visible mix shift inside that line. Those three lines are not the same product: IBKR commissions, Schwab trading, and Robinhood’s payment-for-order-flow, crypto, options, equities, and event stack. Use them as labeled fee-activity dollars — not as a single commission ranking.

Scale: accounts and assets (labeled, not ranked as one unit)

IBKR accounts ended Q2’26 at 5.185M. Schwab active brokerage accounts were 39.802M. Robinhood funded customers were 28.4M (WonderFi newly in the funded count from June 2026). Growth rates favor IBKR. Levels are not interchangeable. Schwab also prints banking and workplace participant accounts and a larger “total client accounts” figure — a different aggregate again, and not the count used here.

Assets tell the same story with bigger gaps. IBKR customer equity $930.3B. Schwab total client assets $13.0849T. Robinhood total platform assets $369B (crypto, event futures, RIA TradePMR non-custody scope, WonderFi crypto from June 2026). Franchise scale on the disclosed prints is Schwab first, then IBKR, then Robinhood — but only as labeled scale color, not as a clean assets league table.

Where the scoreboard breaks: intensity and activity

IBKR still discloses commission per cleared order at $2.64 (a gentle glide versus $2.65 a year ago) and a derived commissions-per-account near $130. Schwab discloses revenue per trade at $1.64 (trading revenue divided by DATs times trading days) — down versus $2.03 a year ago as volumes spiked. Robinhood does not print a dollars-per-trade figure. It prints annualized ARPU $187 and, for this quarter, record equity notional ($956B) plus options contracts (774M).

Daily activity is the sharpest break. IBKR DARTs 4.824M. Schwab DATs 11.92M. Robinhood: no DARTs and no DATs — notional and contracts instead. A combined activity chart would invent a peer unit. This page refuses that. Each panel below keeps its own axis. Robinhood’s notional and contracts are a Q2’26 print only. Earlier quarters stay blank.

So fee intensity versus peers is mixed-to-blocked. IBKR’s own intensity work still stands on the issuer’s commission per order and commissions per account. The peer intensity ranking does not survive the definition mismatches.
