Spotify is not a banner-ad shop waiting for ChatGPT to replace headphones. It is an audio marketplace. It pulls a large global audience into one product, turns that attention into paid subscriptions and into ads on the free tier, and compounds distribution advantages that music rights alone cannot explain.

Two reportable revenue engines share that front door: Premium (paid subscriptions) and Ad-Supported (ads on free). The user cohorts overlap — MAU, Premium, Ad-Supported — and never stack to a total. The metrics below are the scoreboard that decides whether the SaaSpocalypse narrative is earned.

1. What Spotify is and how it makes money

Spotify Technology S.A. runs a consumer audio platform. Revenue shows up in two reportable engines:

  • Premium — subscription revenue from paid users.
  • Ad-Supported — advertising on the free tier.

Premium is still the dollar core. Ad-Supported is the funnel and the smaller absolute line. When the company prints segment totals, Premium plus Ad add to total revenue — so stacked, 100%, and grouped views are all honest for the mix chart. User counts are not additive the same way.

In the second quarter of 2026 the company printed €4.78B total revenue, €4.33B Premium, €446M Ad, operating income €655M, and net income €545M. Free cash flow in the quarter was €797M. Survival is not the open question. Audience durability, Premium price/mix, and the margin path are.

Revenue, operating income, and operating margin sit side by side. They are never stacked.

Fourth-quarter segment and P&L points that are built as FY minus Q1–Q3 are labeled derived and hatched on the chart — including Q4’24 and Q4’25 Premium, Ad, revenue, and operating income where used. Q4 Premium ARPU stays blank: a full-year average is not a fourth-quarter ARPU.

2. Growth engine vs legacy core

Think of the stack as two stories sharing one front door.

Legacy core — Premium. Still the largest dollar engine (Q2’26 Premium €4.33B). Inside it, monetization is carrying through ARPU (€4.89 in Q2’26 versus €4.57 a year earlier) while subscriber year-over-year growth has cooled versus the mid-teens pace of early 2025. That price-and-mix story inside the core is the growth story in the dollars — not a separate segment line.

Growth / funnel engine — Ad-Supported MAUs and Ad revenue. Ad-Supported MAUs grew +14.1% year over year to 494M in Q2’26, faster than Premium’s +8.7% to 300M. Ad revenue itself printed €446M, a touch soft versus €453M a year earlier — so the funnel is still expanding while the free-tier dollar print is the softer watch item this quarter.

Format optionality — podcasts and audiobooks — is thesis color. Hours and share of listening are null across the entire content KPI file. That is a narrative gap, not a chart we fake.

3a. Audience — the primary falsifier lens

Second-quarter 2026: 777M MAUs, 300M Premium, 494M Ad-Supported, versus 696 / 276 / 433 a year earlier. Year over year that is +11.6% / +8.7% / +14.1%. Quarter over quarter net adds were +16M / +7M / +11M.

MAU year-over-year has sat in a roughly eleven-to-twelve percent band for eight quarters — seasonal noise and a soft comparison, not a sustained audience collapse. Premium growth has slowed versus H1’25; Ad is the faster top of funnel. That wedge is the live tension for the long-only thesis.

Premium divided by MAU is ~38.6%. That is stock mix, not conversion. True free-to-paid conversion stays off this pack.

3b. Premium core — ARPU carrying the dollars

Premium ARPU printed €4.89 in Q2’26 (Q1 €4.76; Q2’25 €4.57). Premium revenue €4.33B versus €3.74B a year earlier. Price and mix are doing work on the paid side while subscriber year-over-year decelerates. Annual Premium ARPU on FY stubs is a year average — leave Q4 ARPU blank rather than invent.

3c. Ad-Supported — funnel up, dollars watched

Ad-Supported MAUs are the cleaner growth line on the user scoreboard. Ad revenue remains a smaller absolute line and was slightly down year over year in Q2’26. Do not paper over that with podcast-hour claims the cabinet does not have.

3d. Optional geo color

Annual MAU mix by region (Europe, Latin America, North America, Rest of World) is a clean 100% stack from FY2018–FY2025. Rest of World has been the rising share. No quarterly geo, no geo Premium, no geo ARPU in the cabinet.

Method & definitional notes
  • Q4 Premium, Ad, revenue, operating income, operating margin, and FCF built as FY minus Q1–Q3 are DERIVED and hatched. Q4 Premium ARPU stays blank.
  • MAU, Premium, and Ad-Supported are overlapping cohorts. Premium÷MAU (~38.6% in Q2’26) is stock mix, not free-to-paid conversion.
  • Q4 MAU / Premium / Ad levels are not in this extract and are not invented.
  • Weekly trailing PE is complete weeks only. Loss-era weeks (non-positive TTM) are blank breaks, not an interpolated trend. Thin-earnings weeks stay on the line and are marked.
  • Forward FY1/FY2 is the CLEAR official-current week ending 2026-09-20 only (~44.0× / ~35.1×). NTM is blank. Earlier forward weeks in the source file are history_null and are not drawn.
  • Price is USD and earnings are EUR. No FX translation. pe_ttm is not pe_fy. There is no dual-axis price-plus-PE chart. The single-day Monday print near $511 / ~32× is not the CLEAR week.
  • Podcast and audiobook hours and share of listening are null in every period. No chart.
  • Quarterly cash is sparse in the cabinet and is omitted. FY2026 geo is not annual yet.
  • Non-additive: MAU, Premium, and Ad-Supported overlap. Never stack them. Premium÷MAU is stock mix, not conversion.
