Is enterprise value / GAAP operating income a cleaner valuation lens for Uber than trailing PE, given net income noise from equity marks and tax items — and does EV / adjusted EBITDA, once it is locked from investor relations, confirm the same picture?

The live key-metrics letter already shows what Uber does, how money flows through gross bookings into GAAP revenue, the multi-year income path, free cash flow, and trailing weekly price-to-earnings (latest bound week about 15.3×). That PE chart is honest about early blank weeks and leaves forward PE blank. What it cannot settle is whether the earnings in the denominator are the right earnings for valuation.

Uber’s GAAP net income has been a noisy partner to the operating story. Equity-method and investment revaluations, and tax items, can lift or crush the bottom line in a single year without matching income from operations. This page puts enterprise value over GAAP operating income beside that PE read. Adjusted EBITDA comes in only when the investor-relations reconciliation is locked as fact, not guessed. This is one live question beside the key-metrics letter. It is not a second income-and-PE letter.

Operating income and net income

Read the annual path in plain dollars (full years, GAAP).

From 2017 through 2022, Uber lost money at the operating line every year — about $4.1 billion, $3.0 billion, $8.6 billion, $4.9 billion, $3.8 billion, and $1.8 billion of operating losses in sequence. Net income did not always rhyme. In 2018 the operating loss was about $3.0 billion while net income printed roughly flat at zero. In 2021 the operating loss was still about $3.8 billion, but net income was only about a $0.5 billion loss. In 2022 the operating loss had narrowed to about $1.8 billion, yet net income was a $9.1 billion loss.

The turn is clearer at the operating line. Full-year 2023 delivered about $1.1 billion of income from operations and about $1.9 billion of net income. Then 2024 nearly tripled operating income to about $2.8 billion, while net income jumped to about $9.9 billion. In 2025 operating income roughly doubled again to about $5.6 billion, but net income was almost unchanged at about $10.1 billion.

That is the dig in one paragraph: the operating-income climb from the 2023 turn through 2025 is steep and readable; the net-income path in the same window is large in dollars and much flatter — consistent with investment and tax noise sitting below the operating line. Trailing PE, which leans on that noisy bottom line, can look cheap or dear for reasons that are not mainly about the platform’s operating run-rate. Enterprise value over operating income asks the valuation question on the cleaner operating number.

Enterprise value over operating income

The primary multiple is enterprise value divided by trailing-twelve-month GAAP income from operations.

Enterprise value means equity market capitalization — period-end shares outstanding times the period-end close — plus interest-bearing short- and long-term debt plus disclosed operating and finance lease liabilities, minus cash and equivalents, and minus short-term investments, so the cash subtractor matches the issuer’s unrestricted cash-and-short-term-investments headline. Period-end closes and period-end balance-sheet prints are preferred over daily noise.

When trailing operating income is not positive, the multiple is left blank rather than forced. Net income is never the denominator. Earlier periods with no closing price leave enterprise value blank. The marks on those charts are the blanks. The line does not run through them.

Locked print

As of the quarter ended June 30, 2026:

  • Trailing-twelve-month GAAP operating income $6.700 billion (the four most recent locked quarters).
  • Shares outstanding 2,039,994,000 times the period-end close $72.12 is equity value of about $147.12 billion.
  • Net debt-like items about $9.34 billion. The finance-lease liability was not bound on that quarter, so that leg is counted as zero and labeled, not guessed.
  • Enterprise value $156.464 billion.
  • Enterprise value / operating income about 23.35×.

That sits beside a trailing weekly PE of about 15.3× on the live key-metrics letter (latest bound week). The gap is the dig’s point in one number: the same company looks more expensive on a clean operating-income enterprise multiple than on a net-income PE — consistent with net income running ahead of operating income on investment and tax noise.

Adjusted EBITDA, still open

The second half of the question asks whether enterprise value / adjusted EBITDA confirms the same picture. Uber still prints adjusted EBITDA with a reconciliation in the earnings release (for the second quarter of 2026, about $2.8 billion, up about a third from the year-ago quarter). Management has also said adjusted EBITDA is no longer a key measure, and is moving emphasis toward non-GAAP operating income.

This page does not invent a multi-year adjusted EBITDA series. One current print can sit in the text. A history, and a chart of enterprise value over adjusted EBITDA, wait until the reconciliation is locked period by period. Until then adjusted EBITDA is an open gap, not a shipped multiple. There is no adjusted EBITDA chart on this page.

Open gaps

  1. Adjusted EBITDA history, and enterprise value over adjusted EBITDA, are deferred. One current print is not a history.
  2. Fourth-quarter 2023 operating income is a residual carried from the key-metrics series. It stays labeled and is not replaced with a different figure.
  3. Finance-lease liabilities are denser on the annual balance sheet than on the quarter. An unbound quarter contributes zero, and that zero is labeled.
  4. No forward or next-twelve-month multiple is drawn here, and none is invented to fill the blank on the key-metrics letter.
  5. Gross bookings, trips, and monthly active platform consumers stay on the unit-economics track. They are out of scope for this valuation dig.
