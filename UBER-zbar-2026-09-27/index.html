The short version

Uber matches consumers with independent drivers and couriers, and merchants and shippers with the people who move goods: rides, local delivery, and freight brokerage, in more than seventy countries. This page shows the consolidated scoreboard — revenue, operating income, net income attributable to Uber, and free cash flow — plus a trailing weekly price-to-earnings line through the week ending September 20, 2026, at 15.29 times. Gross bookings, trips, and monthly active consumers are disclosed and not charted here. This is a first reading of the income and cash numbers, not a thesis.

How it makes money

Drivers and couriers earn on the platform. Uber's take becomes reported revenue: service fees, commissions, and related amounts. Mobility is on-demand rides. Delivery is local commerce — food and other goods moved by couriers. Freight is a digital brokerage between shippers and carriers. Under Uber's own definition, freight gross bookings equal freight revenue. Those three lines are the reportable segments.

Do not treat revenue and gross bookings as the same number. Gross bookings are the dollar value of rides, delivery orders, and freight revenue. Revenue is the slice Uber recognizes. Uber calls that slice revenue margin: revenue divided by gross bookings. Business-model and accounting changes can move revenue year over year even when gross bookings are steady. The rate itself is not on this page.

Equity-method and other investment revaluations can swing reported net income and diluted earnings per share in a single quarter without matching the operating-income path. Read net income with that noise in mind. Operating income and free cash flow are the cleaner reads on operations and cash.

Free cash flow

Free cash flow here means operating cash flow minus purchases of property and equipment — the identity Uber states. Annual free cash flow is that identity applied to the annual cash figures. Recent quarterly rows that match the second-quarter 2026 earnings supplemental are the issuer's own reconciliation.

Latest quarter (second quarter of 2026): operating cash flow $2.862B minus purchases of property and equipment $0.070B equals free cash flow $2.792B.

Trailing twelve months ended June 30, 2026: free cash flow $10.116B. That is the sum of the four quarterly free-cash-flow prints from the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, and it matches the supplemental headline.

Clean annual free cash flow runs from 2017 through 2025. Full-year 2025 was $9.763B (operating cash flow $10.099B minus purchases of $0.336B) — about +42% versus 2024's $6.895B. The path runs from deep negatives through 2021, a first positive year in 2022 ($0.390B), then a steep climb through 2023–2025. Purchases of property and equipment stay modest next to operating cash flow. The cash story is operating-cash generation.

Valuation

Trailing weekly price-to-earnings is weekly volume-weighted average price divided by trailing-twelve-month diluted earnings per share, for complete weeks only. Weeks before trailing net income was positive are left blank rather than filled. There is no forward multiple on this chart. No sourced next-twelve-month earnings print is in hand, and this letter does not invent one. When one exists, it belongs as a dotted line on this same chart — not a separate chart, and not price beside the multiple.

The latest complete week ended September 20, 2026: trailing weekly price-to-earnings 15.29×. The chart opens on about the last twelve months, where the multiple has ranged from about 9× to about 18×. Every bound week back to the week ending October 8, 2023 is on the same chart if you zoom out. In early 2024, trailing earnings were still about $0.50 a share and the multiple printed above 100×. Those weeks stay in the series. They are not smoothed.

Open gaps

No long thesis and no price target. The holes below are things this page does not pretend to settle.

Gross bookings, trips, monthly active platform consumers, and revenue margin are disclosed in Uber's investor materials. They are the right next cut for a note on unit economics. They are not drawn here, and they are not filled in from memory.

Segment revenue, segment gross bookings, and segment operating income — Mobility, Delivery, and Freight — are disclosed and not on this page. Geographic revenue, across the United States and Canada, Latin America, Europe-Middle East-Africa, and Asia Pacific, sits in the annual report and was not pulled into this series.

A separate fourth-quarter 2023 operating-income print is not in this file. The bar on the quarterly chart is the full year minus the first three quarters, and it is marked derived. It is not a stand-in we are treating as its own filing line.

Adjusted EBITDA is still reconciled in the earnings supplement, and management has moved its primary non-GAAP operating measure. Neither adjusted EBITDA nor that newer non-GAAP operating income is charted here.

A forward multiple needs a next-twelve-month or next-fiscal-year earnings print with a source. Until that exists, the trailing chart has no dotted segment.

The third quarter of 2026 has not been reported. Nothing on this page waits for it.
