Verdict

Zillow frames housing as hard for a general-purpose assistant to disrupt, and leans on content, context, and integration.

The front door is soft, not collapsing. Average monthly unique users went from 231 million in the second quarter of 2024 to 239 million in the second quarter of 2026, after 243 million a year earlier. Visits on that same-quarter compare went from 2,495 million to 2,529 million. Preferred’s share of connections went from 21% at year-end 2024 to 49% in the first quarter of 2026. Direct traffic is still printed near 80%.

Listing-data access remains a risk the 2025 annual report prints. A lawsuit is not this page. Language-model share of housing search is blank. Open.

Content, context, and integration

Zillow’s printed answer is not a toll on someone else’s assistant. The letters name three things a horizontal model does not already hold: inventory and the rich media on it; months of high-intent use across a move; and touring, financing, and the agent’s work inside one experience. Housing is local, regulated, and rare. The company says that combination is difficult for a general-purpose assistant to walk through.

The ChatGPT app, from October 2025, is described as another door into that experience. AI mode, about 5% of the audience in the first quarter of 2026, is an assistant on Zillow. On the data itself, the letters point at ShowingTime on about 90% of U.S. for-sale tours, Follow Up Boss on most high-volume teams, agents using at least one Zillow product on an estimated 80% of transactions, the Zestimate history, and a rentals business with no MLS. The 2025 annual report still lists relationships with listing and data providers, timely access to listing data and its quality, and MLS rules as risks. The letters are fuller on the product story than on any measured share of search that now starts in an assistant.
