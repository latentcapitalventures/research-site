The short version

Amazon is a retailer, marketplace, advertising, subscription, and cloud company under one roof. Customers buy goods on Amazon’s own sites and apps; third-party sellers pay for fulfillment and selling services; advertisers buy sponsored placements; subscribers pay for Prime and related memberships; and enterprises rent compute, storage, and related cloud services from AWS. In its filings Amazon reports three segments that carry their own operating income: North America, International, and AWS. The business map we care about — Online stores, Third-party seller services, Advertising, Subscriptions, and AWS — mostly shows up as product-line slices of consolidated net sales, not as separate profit lines. AWS is the overlap: it is both a reportable segment and a product-line line. This pack does not yet have locked dollar series for those segment or product-line cuts, so mix and leverage by line stay open. What we can lock today is the consolidated scoreboard: net sales, operating income, net income, diluted earnings per share, and free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capital spending) where the filings print them, plus a trailing weekly price-to-earnings spine from complete ISO weeks. Forward earnings multiples stay blank. This is a first key-metrics letter after the numbers — not a locked thesis.

How it makes money

Online stores and third-party seller services are the retail engine: Amazon sells goods itself and takes fees when other sellers use its marketplace and fulfillment. Advertising is sponsored placements and related ad products sold into that traffic. Subscriptions are Prime and related memberships. AWS is the cloud business sold to enterprises — and the one product line that also appears as its own reportable segment with its own operating income.

North America and International roll up the retail, advertising, and subscription activity by geography. Only those two geographies plus AWS print segment operating income. Do not read a separate operating-income line for Online stores, Advertising, Subscriptions, or Third-party seller services off this pack — Amazon does not allocate profit that way.

Free cash flow

Here free cash flow means operating cash flow minus capital spending, labeled by window. Amazon’s investor materials often headline a trailing-twelve-month figure; that tagged trailing series is not in our locked set yet. Every quarterly free-cash-flow row in this pack is a single quarter: operating cash flow minus capital spending equals the free-cash-flow print.

Latest quarter with free cash flow (first quarter of 2026): operating cash flow $26.032B minus capital spending $44.203B equals free cash flow −$18.171B. Capital spending is elevated; the quarter burns cash on this definition. Second-quarter 2026 free cash flow is not yet in the locked series.

Clean annual free cash flow runs 2016 through 2025. Full-year 2025 free cash flow was $20.810B (operating cash flow $113.903B minus capital spending $93.093B) — an operating-cash-flow-minus-capex annual print, not Amazon’s trailing-twelve-month headline. Full-year 2024 free cash flow was $48.340B. Earlier years show the investment cycle — deep negatives in 2021–2022, then recovery in 2023–2024, then another step-down in 2025 as capital spending rose. Full-year 2026 cash tags stay off the locked annual chart.

Latest complete week (week 38 of 2026, ended September 20, 2026): weekly VWAP about $250.35, trailing-twelve-month diluted EPS about $12.41 (four quarters through June 30, 2026), trailing weekly PE about 20.2×.

Bound PE weeks run from April 6, 2025 (week 14 of 2025, about 29.8×) through that latest week. Across those 77 weeks the multiple runs roughly 19× to 36×. Earlier weeks in the price file (back to August 2020) have VWAP but no PE yet — four quarters of locked net income for the trailing sum were not in the extract for those dates, so those weeks are skipped on the chart rather than invented.

The daily price series shows the 20-for-1 split break in June 2022 (weekly VWAP drops from about $2,450 to about $120 across adjacent weeks). That break sits in the null-PE window, so it does not bend the PE line; it still belongs in the caption so nobody reads pre-split and post-split prices as one continuous level.

Forward price-to-earnings stays blank until there is a locked street or company next-twelve-month earnings print. When one lands, house rule is a dotted overlay on the trailing chart — never a separate forward chart, and never price and PE on two axes.

Older point snapshots in the valuation bag (about 59.7× on 2024 net income alone, or about 65.5× at a higher price) are not this weekly spine. The chart above is weekly VWAP over trailing-twelve-month diluted EPS; those point prints use a different denominator and date and should not be read against the line.
