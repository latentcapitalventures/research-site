The short version

Booking Holdings is a global online travel company. Travelers use Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK, and OpenTable to find and book stays and other travel. Booking earns commissions and related fees when those bookings happen. The stock has been weak. One reading is that markets are pricing an AI scare that may overstate the damage to the business. This first pack does not settle that debate. It puts the scoreboard on the table: how revenue and earnings have grown, and where the shares trade on trailing earnings. Operating scale metrics such as room nights and gross bookings are still missing and are labeled as gaps.

How it makes money

The company sits between travelers and hotels, airlines, restaurants, and other travel suppliers. It takes a cut when a booking is completed — sometimes collecting payment itself, sometimes letting the supplier collect — and it also earns advertising and metasearch fees. Brand-by-brand profit and the merchant versus agency mix are not yet numbers here, so the brand list is context, not a segment chart.

Growth — revenue and earnings

After the pandemic rebound, revenue has kept compounding. Full-year 2025 revenue was $26.9 billion, up 13% from 2024. Operating income rose with it, to $8.83 billion. Net income did not: it fell 8% to $5.40 billion. That gap between a stronger operating line and a weaker bottom line is the clearest scoreboard fact on the annual series.

The latest reported quarter is second-quarter 2026: revenue $7.35 billion, operating income $2.50 billion, net income $1.95 billion.

Valuation

Trailing price-to-earnings uses weekly volume-weighted average price divided by trailing twelve-month earnings per share, adjusted for the 25-for-1 split that took effect April 2, 2026. For the week of September 14–20, 2026, that multiple was 18.2× (price about $171, trailing EPS about $9.36). The weekly series runs from late 2015 through that week with no holes in the middle.

The chart shows trailing PE for about the last twelve months. Forward PE is omitted this pass — there is no locked guidance midpoint or named consensus print. Weeks in 2020–2022 when trailing earnings nearly vanished still show extreme multiples on the full weekly series; those points stay flagged, not smoothed. They are outside the twelve-month window drawn here.
