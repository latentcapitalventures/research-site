How it makes money

Zillow Group runs consumer brands — Zillow, Trulia, StreetEasy, HotPads — that pull demand for homes and apartments. Revenue shows up in three engines:

  1. Residential — Premier Agent and Preferred-style connection advertising, plus related for-sale marketplace products.
  2. Mortgages — including Zillow Home Loans (ZHL) purchase origination.
  3. Rentals — multifamily and rental marketplace revenue.

From fourth-quarter 2024 on, the company also prints a For Sale subtotal (Residential plus Mortgages). Rentals stands alone. Earlier periods now carry For Sale as a filing-backed print where the issuer printed it; the only modern chart points still built as Residential plus Mortgages (and marked derived) are first through third quarter 2023. Homes-era labels are not For Sale.

Resid, Mort, and Rentals as themselves — not a pre-2023 relabel.

For Sale continuous from early 2023; derived watermark only on Q1–Q3’23. Rentals is the second locomotive on the same front door.

In second-quarter 2026 the company printed $772M total revenue, $549M For Sale, $209M Rentals, Adjusted EBITDA $176M, and a slight GAAP loss (−$4M). Cash was $572M. Survival is not the open question. Mix quality and take-rate are — and those sit on the unit-econ dig.

Three series side by side. Adjusted EBITDA stays on the recent, scale-safe window; total revenue and GAAP net income can run longer.

Audience — soft, not collapsing

Q2’26 visits 2,529M / AMUU 239 versus Q2’25 2,590M / 243 — soft year-over-year, not a sustained collapse. Fourth-quarter quarterly traffic points are blank on our series (letters print them); leave those cells quiet rather than invent.

Using Comscore figures the company itself quotes, Zillow Group held about 70% of the real-estate audience in both first- and second-quarter 2026. That is the sharper front-door print than raw AMUU alone.
